# Platform Eatability (Phase 4)

Question: could a frontier agent/model vendor absorb the value as an
ordinary platform feature? Verified live: provider consoles already expose
token usage, rate limits, spend caps, and workspace limits — cost telemetry
is native to them. What they structurally lack: every business fact
(CSAT, refunds, stages, incidents, merges — none passes through inference).

## Classification: PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT (uniformly, all domains)

- EASILY_PLATFORM_ABSORBED: nothing proposed. No vendor can calculate
  case/incident/invoice/lead/task economics without independent business
  systems, and multi-provider jobs defeat any single-provider record.
- The independence that exists (provider-blindness to business truth) does
  NOT create Thompson value, because incumbents + warehouse already cover
  the join. Independence from the executor matters for audit/procurement in
  principle, but the data to audit with lives in systems the customer
  already owns and can already query.
- Longitudinal cross-provider history (the hypothesized moat) compounds
  only if the joined episode is itself uniquely valuable — killed in Phase 3.
  Stored traces are not a moat (cold-start analysis concurs).

Vendor-capture risk is LOW and irrelevant: nobody needs to capture what a
warehouse already answers. The threat that kills this thesis is not the
platform — it is the incumbent database plus ordinary joins.
